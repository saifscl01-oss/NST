What the two charts mean together

The same label, the same access, two opposite outcomes

CARBON and 4 BRICKS DOWN are both sauvachi/NST joint releases, roughly a month apart in age, both carrying huge Discovery-Mode-driven historical lift numbers (+9,052% and +184% respectively, per the campaign dashboard). If DM enrollment alone explained momentum, they'd track together. They don't.

The mechanism this implies: Discovery Mode amplifies the save momentum a track already has walking in — it doesn't manufacture momentum a track doesn't have. CARBON's saves were still accelerating in the months before DM enrollment expanded portfolio-wide (Jan–Mar '26); 4 BRICKS DOWN's saves had already started falling before that same expansion. Same lever, opposite starting conditions, opposite results.

What this means for Halos

A month-3 test, not a week-1 verdict

01

Ignore the week-1 saves/listener ratio — it's structurally suppressed

CARBON's own release-window ratio (0.33%) was the lowest of its entire first year, well below every later month. The first algorithmic push floods in low-intent listeners before the song settles to its real audience. Don't read early softness as a bad sign on its own.

02

Track absolute monthly saves for months 1–4. One dip is normal — two isn't

Every track here dipped once (CARBON: month 5, −17%; money gene: month 2, −61%) and rebounded. 4 BRICKS DOWN dipped in month 3 and never recovered. If Halos's saves are still falling by the start of month 4, treat it as a one-and-done track rather than waiting on Discovery Mode to fix it.

03

Get NST's distributor to flip on DM access before that month-3 test resolves

If Halos passes the rebound test, the payoff only lands if DM enrollment can start immediately — not after another 2–4 week approval cycle. Enrollment decisions run 2–4 weeks ahead of each monthly campaign (the August cohort was locked Jul 11), so this needs to be in motion now, independent of how Halos performs.

04

Budget for a ceiling, not just a floor, once enrolled

63–69% of even a year-old proven track's monthly volume is now DM-sourced. That's not a temporary lift to bank once and move on from — it's the channel Halos will depend on for the majority of its long-run reach, the same way CARBON still does eleven months later.

Read this as directional, not statistical. Three tracks, two with a full year of history — enough to see a real, mechanistically coherent pattern, not enough to treat the month-3 threshold as a law. Re-run this exact monthly pull after Halos's first four months and the sample doubles.
